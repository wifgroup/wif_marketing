Campaign focus

State targeting and metro targeting are not interchangeable settings.

Most recoverable waste in an Australian account is not in the keyword list. It sits in location settings that were never split, budgets that treat every state as equally winnable, ad copy written without regard for what can be substantiated, and conversion setups that cannot tell a booked job from a tyre-kicker.

Geography

A state is a billing region, not a market.

Selecting New South Wales as a target does not mean competing in one auction. Sydney, Newcastle, Wollongong and regional NSW behave as separate markets with different competitor sets and different costs to win a click. When they share a campaign budget, the cheapest region absorbs delivery and the metro that matters most gets starved. WIF separates the regions that need their own budget line from those that can safely share.

Budget planning

An AUD budget has to be told which market it belongs to.

The common planning error is committing a national budget to a national campaign and hoping coverage sorts itself out. A modest monthly budget spread across every capital produces too little data in each to support a confident decision anywhere. It is usually more productive to concentrate on one or two metros, gather enough conversion volume to read a genuine signal, then expand. WIF plans budgets against the decisions they can support, not against a coverage map.

Competitive reality

Established local agencies are already in these auctions.

Australian paid search is a mature market. In most commercial categories the top positions belong to advertisers who have bid for years, accumulated conversion history and put local proof on their landing pages. That does not make the auction unwinnable, but a new account rarely wins by outbidding it.

Ad copy compliance

Claims in ad copy need something behind them.

Australian Consumer Law treats advertising claims as representations, and the obligation not to mislead applies to a search-ad headline the same way it applies to a brochure. Superlatives, guarantees, price claims and "no hidden fees" promises all need substantiation the business can produce. WIF flags claims the client cannot evidence and rewrites toward specifics that are both defensible and more persuasive, since a concrete statement usually outperforms a vague superlative anyway.

Seasonality

The southern hemisphere calendar changes when demand arrives.

Australian seasonality runs opposite to the northern hemisphere patterns most global playbooks assume. Cooling, outdoor work and summer trades peak in the months a northern calendar treats as off-season, and heating demand arrives mid-year. Layered on top is December-January, when much of the country moves into holidays, decision-makers are unavailable and B2B enquiry slows even where consumer categories stay busy. Budgets should be set against that calendar in advance.

Lead capture

Calls and forms are different leads and deserve different tracking.

For many Australian service businesses the phone remains the primary conversion, particularly for urgent work. If calls and form fills are counted as one undifferentiated conversion, bidding optimises toward whichever is easier to generate rather than whichever produces revenue. WIF separates the two events, checks that call tracking survives the click through to the landing page, and feeds back which enquiries became quoted work.

Process

How WIF starts a Google Ads audit.

The audit is diagnostic before it is prescriptive. The first job is to work out whether the account is losing money on geography, query quality, the landing page or what happens after the enquiry arrives — four problems that look identical in a cost-per-conversion report but need different fixes.

  • Review account structure, match types, search terms and negative keyword coverage.
  • Audit location settings, including whether presence or interest targeting is in use and which regions share a budget.
  • Separate national demand from state and metro intent, and identify which splits the budget can support.
  • Check conversion events for call and form separation, duplication and genuine buyer intent.
  • Read ad copy against what the business can substantiate, and flag claims that create risk.
  • Map landing pages against Australian buyer intent, service-area clarity and offer specificity.
  • Review lead quality, source context and how quickly enquiries are being followed up.
  • Build a first 30-day action plan sequencing the fixes before any budget increase.

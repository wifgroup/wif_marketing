Short answer

What a tracking setup has to prove before you trust the report.

The test is simple. Take one lead in the CRM and say which campaign, ad group and keyword produced it, whether sales called it, and what happened next. Most accounts fail somewhere between the click and the CRM record.

GA4 conversion events

A short list of events that mean a real enquiry.

For lead generation that is a confirmed form submission, a completed booking, a tracked call over a minimum duration and a chat that actually starts. Button clicks, scroll depth and page views stay off the conversion list, because once a soft event counts it flows into smart bidding and the platform buys the wrong behaviour.

Event validation

Every event is tested before it can influence bidding.

We watch firing in real time, submit test leads end to end, confirm nothing double-fires on refresh, and compare GA4, ad platform and CRM counts for the same window. Periods when an event was broken get marked, so a bad month is not later read as a real trend.

UTM governance

One naming convention, applied everywhere, reviewed weekly.

Inconsistent UTMs are the most common reason attribution collapses. We fix the vocabulary for source and medium, force lower case, build links from a shared template and cover paid, email, WhatsApp, partner and QR traffic. Start from the UTM tracking template for paid ads.

Source into the CRM

Campaign context has to travel with the lead.

UTM values and the platform click identifier are captured into hidden form fields, stored on the lead record and preserved through routing and stage changes. That is what lets a sales owner see the source before the first call, and what makes cost per qualified lead reportable at all.

Call tracking

Phone enquiries are counted, not estimated.

In most Indian lead-gen accounts a large share of enquiries arrive by phone. We track click-to-call on mobile, use dynamic number insertion where volume justifies it, set a minimum duration so wrong numbers do not count, and route call records into the CRM with the same source fields as forms.

Consent Mode

Consent handling wired into the tags, not bolted on.

Where consent rules apply to your audience, the banner has to talk to Consent Mode so tags adjust behaviour instead of firing regardless. That changes what you see rather than breaking it: denied consent produces modelled conversions, and knowing which part of a number is modelled is part of reading the report honestly.

Offline conversions

Send the outcome back to the platform that bought the click.

For B2B, high-ticket and showroom businesses a form fill is not the outcome. Using the click identifier captured at submission, qualified-lead and closed-deal statuses are imported back into Google Ads and Meta so bidding optimises toward enquiries that became customers rather than toward easy form fillers.

Lead-quality feedback

Sales feedback belongs inside the optimisation loop.

Qualification status, disqualification reason and stage movement are recorded against the source, then read alongside search terms, audiences and creatives. A campaign with a cheap cost per lead and a poor qualified rate should lose budget even when the platform dashboard looks healthy.

Dashboard and reporting

One weekly view, built for one decision.

Spend, leads, qualified leads, cost per lead, cost per qualified lead, follow-up speed, stage movement and revenue influenced, each traceable to a campaign.

Tracking workflow

How WIF rebuilds a tracking and attribution setup.

Sequence matters. Fixing dashboards before fixing events only produces a tidier version of the same wrong number, so the data path is repaired from the click end forward.

  • Audit existing events, tags, UTMs and CRM fields against real submitted test leads.
  • Define the conversion list and remove soft events from bidding signals.
  • Publish one UTM standard and rebuild every live link to match it.
  • Capture source and click identifiers into the CRM on every form and call.
  • Import qualified-lead and revenue outcomes back into the ad platforms.
  • Run one weekly report and make a documented budget decision from it.

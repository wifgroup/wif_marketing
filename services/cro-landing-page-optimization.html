Short answer

What a landing page optimization review covers before more traffic is added.

Most pages do not lose buyers because of design. They lose buyers because the page answers a different question than the ad asked, asks for more than the offer deserves, or hides the proof that would settle the reader's objection.

Message match

The ad promise should be the first readable line on the page.

When the ad sells a delivered price and the page opens with company history, the visitor has to translate before acting and a share of them will not. We map each ad group to the section that answers it, keep the buyer's own wording, and split pages asked to serve two unlike intents.

Offer construction

An offer hierarchy, not one undifferentiated submit button.

Buyers arrive at different distances from a decision, so a page needs a primary action for the ready ones and a lower-commitment path for the rest. A quote, a site visit and a guide download are not interchangeable. We pick the one the campaign is buying and demote the others.

Objections

Write the page against the reasons buyers say no.

Price uncertainty, delivery time, whether you serve their city, minimum order, who actually calls back. Sales answers these every day and the page usually does not. We collect them from the sales team and answer each one where it occurs to the reader, not in a footer FAQ.

Proof placement

Proof belongs where the doubt is, not in a logo strip.

A named client example, a photo of real work or a specific project detail carries weight when it sits directly beneath the claim it supports. We place proof at the hero, beside the offer and near the form, and keep detailed engagements in the case studies.

Form friction

Every field has to earn its place on the first call.

If sales does not use a field before speaking to the lead, it is friction. Qualification questions usually belong in the callback or with an AI calling agent instead. We check field count, label clarity, mobile keyboard types, error handling and whether the button says what happens next.

Mobile speed

Judge the page on a throttled mobile profile.

Most paid traffic in India arrives on mobile, often on an uneven connection. What matters is how long the headline, offer and first CTA take to become usable, not a desktop score. Sliders, autoplay video, chat widgets on first paint and unused scripts are the usual causes.

Thank-you page

The page after submission is part of the conversion.

A confirmation screen should name who will call and when, hand over an immediate next step such as a booking link or direct number, and deliver anything promised. It is also where the conversion event and source parameters get recorded so the lead can be matched to a campaign in the CRM.

Testing discipline

Most small accounts lack the traffic for a valid A/B test.

A split test needs enough conversions per variant to separate a real difference from ordinary weekly noise, and an account producing a few dozen leads a month will not reach that in a sensible window. We run sequential changes instead: one at a time, held for a full buying cycle.

Lead quality

A page that converts more is not automatically better.

Loosening an offer or stripping a form can lift submissions while sending sales a worse pipeline, so every change is read against qualified rate and CRM stage movement before it is locked in.

Optimization workflow

How WIF sequences landing page changes.

Changes are ordered by the evidence behind them and how fast they can ship, so the highest-confidence fixes land before anything is rebuilt.

  • Audit the ad-to-page path for message match, offer clarity and unanswered objections.
  • Cut form friction and fix mobile experience before touching layout or visual design.
  • Place proof next to the claims and objections it actually resolves.
  • Rebuild the thank-you page to set expectations and record clean conversion data.
  • Run one meaningful change at a time and hold it for a full buying cycle.
  • Review qualified leads and sales feedback, not page conversion rate on its own.
